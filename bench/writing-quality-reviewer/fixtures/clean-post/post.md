# Stop estimating. Start splitting.

Eight months ago my team of five stopped using story points. We count tickets per week now, and that's it.

I didn't drop points because of some theory. I dropped them because every planning meeting had turned into a forty-minute argument about whether a ticket was a 3 or a 5.

The obvious worry is that counting tickets throws away information. A big ticket and a small one both count as one, so surely the forecast gets worse? I checked. Over six months, our points-based forecast missed by about 30%. Counting tickets missed by about 25%. It isn't worse, and we got those forty minutes back.

Here's the catch, and it's the whole trick: it only works if your tickets are roughly the same size. So we split. Every ticket gets broken down until it's about a day or two of work. If a ticket is wildly bigger than the rest, the count lies, and you split before you count.

That's where the skill actually is. Not in guessing whether something is a 3 or a 5, but in cutting the work into pieces small enough that the guess stops mattering.

Stop estimating. Start splitting.
