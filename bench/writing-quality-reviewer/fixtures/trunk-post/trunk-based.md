# Why Your Team Should Move to Trunk-Based Development

Think of your codebase as a highway. Every long-lived branch is a car parked in the fast lane.

Trunk-based development represents a fundamental shift in how engineering organizations approach collaboration. The adoption of short-lived branches enables faster feedback across the development lifecycle. Integration friction decreases significantly when changes are merged frequently into a shared mainline.

Continuous integration practices benefit substantially from this model. The reduction in merge complexity allows pipelines to provide more meaningful signals. Test suites become more reliable indicators of overall system health when the codebase converges regularly.

Feature flags play an important role in this approach. The separation of deployment from release gives organizations greater flexibility. Incomplete functionality can remain hidden while still being integrated into the main codebase.

Code review processes also evolve under trunk-based development. Smaller changes facilitate more thorough evaluation. Reviewer fatigue diminishes when the scope of each change remains manageable.

Research supports these benefits. According to industry data, 73% of elite-performing teams use trunk-based development, and teams that adopt it see a 40% reduction in lead time.

Cultural alignment is another important consideration. Trust between team members grows when everyone works from the same source of truth. Collaboration improves as visibility into ongoing work increases.

In conclusion, trunk-based development offers a compelling path forward for modern engineering teams. By embracing this approach, organizations can unlock faster delivery, higher quality, and stronger collaboration.
