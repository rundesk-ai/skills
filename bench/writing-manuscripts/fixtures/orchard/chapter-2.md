# The Orchard

Hannah walked out into the orchard. She saw that the apples had started to fall. She felt the cold of the morning on her face. She could hear the crows in the far trees. She noticed that the gate at the bottom had been left open again, and she felt annoyed, because she had told her father twice about the gate.

The orchard had been in the family for three generations. Her great-grandfather had planted the first trees in 1931, after he came back from the city with nothing, and her grandmother had added the pear trees along the wall, and her father had let all of it go, slowly, a little more every year since her mother died.

She walked down to the gate. Her father was standing there. He was thinking that she would be angry about the gate.

"You left it open," she said.

"The deer don't come anymore," he said.

She shut it anyway and leaned on it and looked back up the slope at the house and the trees and the rotting fruit in the grass and the one light on in the kitchen window and thought that if she stayed one more winter she would never leave, never, and that maybe that was the thing she was most afraid of and also the thing she wanted.
