# Nine

Owen came down the cellar stairs slowly, one at a time, because the third one had always creaked and he did not want anyone upstairs to hear him. It was very dark down there, darker than he had expected it to be, and it smelled of damp earth and old potatoes and something else that he could not quite name. He stood at the bottom for a while and let his eyes adjust to the dark.

Somewhere above him a door closed. He held his breath and listened, and after a while he heard footsteps cross the kitchen floor, and then stop, and then go back the way they had come.

He found the torch where Maud had said it would be, on the shelf by the boiler, and he switched it on. The beam was weak and yellow. It caught the edge of the old wardrobe against the far wall, and the rug in front of it, and the small brass key that lay under the corner of the rug, exactly where she had told him it would be.

He picked up the key and put it in his pocket. Then he crossed to the wardrobe. He put his hand on the handle. It was cold. He thought about Maud, and about what she had said to him on the night of the fire, and about whether he believed her.

He opened the wardrobe.
