# Six

It was very late in the afternoon, almost evening really, by the time Iris finally got the attic hatch open, and the light that came down through it was the colour of weak tea. She stood on the third rung of the ladder for a long moment, not moving, just looking up into it, and waited for her eyes to get used to the gloom.

The attic smelled of dust and mice and, faintly, of her mother's lavender, which made no sense at all, because her mother had been dead for nine years and had never once, as far as Iris knew, come up here.

She climbed the rest of the way and knelt on the boards. The trunk was where Dad had said it would be, under the eaves, behind the broken rocking horse with one glass eye. She pulled it towards her. It was heavier than it looked. Inside, on top of everything, wrapped in a tea towel, were her mother's good scissors, the ones nobody was ever allowed to use for paper.

Iris sat back on her heels and did not touch them.
