# Sources

What each source established, and where it shows in the skill. Links verified 2026-11-15.

| Source | Kind | Established | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jeanette Sherman, *Why "Slop" Looks So Sloppy* (essay) | practitioner essay drawing on rhetoric and composition | The framework: model-written text is strong on cohesion, weak on coherence and flat on cadence, and readers notice even when they cannot name it. Coherence is whether the whole builds to a point; abstractions acting as subjects are a reliable tell; stock connective phrases perform transitions without doing them; varied sentence length steers attention; readers attend to what is novel and what serves their purpose. | The three C's and their priority; coherence; actors, not abstractions; stock glue; cadence; novelty and meaning. The procedure, the checks and the debt example were worked out from it in a working guide written for agents; this skill restates them in its own words. |
| Digital.gov, [Writing for understanding](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/writing) (U.S. federal plain-language guidance) | public guidance | "Active voice makes it clear who should do what"; in an active sentence the person or agency acting is the subject; a hidden verb (nominalization) is a verb turned into a noun, and "We analyze data" beats "We conduct an analysis of the data". | Actors, not abstractions; the reader as actor in instructions and short forms. |
| Joseph M. Williams, *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace* (book) | composition textbook | Readers judge prose clear when the main characters are the subjects and their actions are the verbs; abstractions as subjects and actions buried in nouns make prose read as dense and evasive. | Actors, not abstractions; the subject scan. |
