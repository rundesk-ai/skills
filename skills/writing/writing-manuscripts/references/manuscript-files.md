# Manuscript files

Read this when the manuscript is kept as files in a writing app or a project folder, a sheet or
file per chapter or scene, rather than as one document. Apps differ and change, so the app's own
guidance is the authority on how it orders, titles and lays out a book: read it when it's available,
and use what follows where it says nothing.

## Read the writer's structure first

- **Edit only the files you were asked about.** Read the others for continuity and style.
- **Not every folder is the story.** Folders are often chapters or parts and the files in them
  scenes, but writers also keep notes, research, outlines and character sheets. Never assume a
  folder is a chapter; ask when you can't tell whether it belongs to the book. Notes and reference
  sheets are the truth for names and facts, and a disagreement with them is reported, not fixed.
- **Read the book in its order.** Find how the app orders files (an order file it keeps, a list in
  its settings, or name prefixes such as `01-`) and read the chapters around the one you edit in
  that order, not in the folder listing's order.
- **Project rules for documentation don't apply to the manuscript.**

## Change the structure the app's way

- **Never rename a file to reorder or retitle it.** Other files, links and the app may point at the
  name. Reorder through the app's own order (its tool or its order file), and when order comes only
  from name prefixes and the app offers no other way, ask before renaming.
- **A title lives where the app takes it from,** often the file's first line or its first heading.
  Change it there, and leave front matter (the block between `---` lines at the top) as it is.
- **Start a chapter on a new page through structure** where the app lays out pages, for example by
  making the chapter a top-level folder or file, not with a page-break marker typed into the text.
- **Add a scene as a new file in the chapter's folder** at its place in the order. Splitting a long
  chapter into scenes changes the writer's structure: propose it first, then move the text without
  rewording it.
- **When you move a file, fix the links your move broke,** in every file, notes included, changing
  only the link target, and list each change so the writer can undo it. A broken link you didn't
  cause is reported, not fixed.

## Format as the writer does

- **Match the writer's markdown:** their italic marker, their scene-break marker (`***` or `---`),
  their footnotes. Converting one style to another touches every line and changes their file.
- **Add nothing for layout:** no indents, extra blank lines or non-breaking spaces when the app sets
  paragraphs itself; one blank line between paragraphs is enough.
- **A scene break between two files goes at the end of the earlier file** when the first line of a
  file is its title.

## Rundesk

A project in Rundesk keeps its manuscript as sheets in a `pages/` folder (or the folder its
`desk.yml` names) and gives you Rundesk's tools. There, before you reorder, move or retitle a
sheet, call Rundesk's guide with the topic `pages` and follow it: it is the authority and may have
changed since this was written. When the guide isn't available, at the time of writing Rundesk
orders each folder by a hidden `.order` file (one name per line, unlisted names after, by file
name), takes a sheet's title from its first line, and starts a new printed page at each top-level
sheet or folder, so reorder by writing that folder's `.order` rather than asking or renaming.
