# Devlogs

A devlog is the owner's record of making a game. Its readers are players and other developers, and the
reason they read is the process: what was tried, what broke, what got cut, and why. Honesty about failure is
what makes a devlog credible, and it's exactly what AI-polished writing tends to sand off.

## Editing a devlog

- **Keep every failure, cut feature, and open bug.** "Wall slide is still broken" stays in. Don't reframe a
  failure as a win or soften it into "an area for improvement".
- **Problem, attempt, result, next.** That's the natural shape. Order the owner's notes that way when they
  fit it, but don't invent a step the notes don't have.
- **Numbers stay exact.** Tuning values, durations, counts. "0.1s" stays "0.1s"; don't round, convert, or
  add units the owner didn't use. "like 20 times" can stay as "about 20 times", never "20 times" or
  "dozens of times".
- **Don't name what the owner didn't name.** No engine, language, tool, genre, or game title unless it's in
  the notes. If it matters to the reader, query it.
- **Explain jargon only if the owner did, or query it.** Terms like coyote time or jump buffering are fine
  for a game-dev audience. If a one-line explanation would help, ask before adding one, since defining it
  is adding content.
- **Prose, not a changelog.** Tell the week as a short story in the owner's words: what they tried, what
  happened, what they did next. Two or three headings at most, and none is fine. A bullet list only for the
  owner's "next" items.
- **Short is fine.** A devlog doesn't need an intro or a conclusion. End on the owner's "next" list if they
  gave one.
- **Title** follows the owner's numbering ("Devlog #3") plus a short subtitle drawn from the main topic.
- **Media.** If the notes mention a clip or screenshot, leave `[TK: GIF of ...]` where it should go.
