# AI tells to remove

Patterns that make prose read as machine-written, mostly from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing"
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing). Remove them when you see them, and don't
introduce them while editing. They are signs, not proof: a clean piece can still be vague or inflated, so
the trace check matters more than this list.

- **Inflated significance.** "stands as", "a testament to", "pivotal", "plays a crucial role", "setting the
  stage for", "marks a turning point".
- **Trailing "-ing" commentary.** ", highlighting the importance of...", ", showcasing...", ", underscoring...".
- **Promotional or hype words.** "vibrant", "rich", "boasts", "seamless", "powerful", "robust",
  "cutting-edge", "game-changer", "unlock", "leverage".
- **Avoiding plain verbs.** "serves as", "features", "offers" where "is" or "has" would do.
- **AI vocabulary.** "delve", "crucial", "intricate", "tapestry", "landscape", "realm", "navigate",
  "foster", "additionally", "moreover", "furthermore".
- **Negative parallelisms.** "It's not just X, it's Y", "not only... but also". The owner sometimes uses a
  plain "X isn't Y. It's Z." in their own words; keep theirs, don't add more.
- **Reflexive triads.** Lists of three added for rhythm when the owner gave two things or four.
- **Vague attribution.** "experts say", "many developers feel", "studies show" with no source.
- **Hedging.** "it's worth noting", "generally speaking", "in many cases", "arguably". The owner commits.
- **Tidy wrap-ups.** "In conclusion", "Ultimately", "At the end of the day", "Despite these challenges",
  a closing paragraph that restates every section.
- **Punchline closers.** Ending section after section on a short one-line paragraph that sums up what was
  just said ("It doesn't write like people think." then "AI doesn't name things for what they are."). The
  same shape twice reads as a template, and a closer that repeats the heading says nothing new. Fold the
  line into the paragraph above it, or cut it.
- **Elegant variation.** Swapping synonyms to avoid repeating a word. Repeat the owner's term.
- **Formatting tells.** Bolded phrases mid-sentence, bullet lists with bold inline headers where prose
  would do, emoji, title-cased headings everywhere, a heading every couple of paragraphs in a short
  opinion post.
- **Pull quotes.** Lifting a line out of the post into a `>` blockquote for emphasis. The owner thinks it
  looks terrible. Keep the line inside its paragraph. Use `>` only to quote someone else's actual words.
  To break up a dense stretch, use a short list of real examples the owner gave, or split a paragraph.
- **Chatbot residue.** "Here's the revised version", "I hope this helps", "Great question".
- **Em dashes.** Use commas, periods, or parentheses unless the voice guide allows them.
